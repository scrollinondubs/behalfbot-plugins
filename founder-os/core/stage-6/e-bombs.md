# E-bombs: teach before you sell

## Purpose

Earn an audience's trust by fixing their small problems in public and for free, before you ask them to buy anything. E-bombs is Amy Hoy and Alex Hillman's name, from 30x500, for these free fixes. The outcome is a wall of published pieces and an email list whose members write back.

## When to use

Use it at stage 6. By then the ledger should hold a quote-backed pain log from Sales Safari, Hoy and Hillman's method for researching an audience. It should also hold the active watering holes from stage 1. Watering holes is their term for the places online where an audience already gathers.

It is the wrong card if the pain log is thin or built from your own guesses. Go back to the stage 2 card 'Sales Safari: a quote-backed pain log' and read more threads first. If you are drafting a sales page, use 'Pain-Dream-Fix: the page that sells' instead.

## Principles

- An e-bomb leaves the reader better off even if they never hear your name again.
- Every topic comes from a logged pain with real quotes behind it. No quote, no post.
- One post fixes one problem that the reader can act on today.
- Publish where the audience already talks. Don't wait for them to find your blog.
- Count what readers do. A single reply is worth more than a thousand impressions.

## Procedure

1. **Pick the pain.** Rank the pain log by headcount: the number of separate people who complained, not how loudly any one of them did. A pain qualifies once it turns up in three or more unrelated threads. Suppose your reconciliation tool's log has one entry quoted in a bookkeepers' Facebook group, a Reddit thread and a Shopify community post, each person baffled that the deposit hitting the bank is lower than the month's sales. Take that one. Show: the chosen pain entry, linked to the draft.
2. **Name the one thing.** In one sentence, write what the reader will be able to do after reading: "Match a Shopify payout to its orders in a spreadsheet in ten minutes." If it takes two sentences, you have two e-bombs. Show: the one-line promise at the top of the draft.
3. **Write the fix in their words.** The first lines should sound like one of your logged quotes: "payout short again", "fees and refunds netted out", whatever terms the bookkeepers used themselves. From there, go straight to the method - numbered steps, a sheet they can copy, or one real month reconciled line by line. Your tool, your backstory and next quarter's features stay out. Show: a draft a stranger could work through start to finish without messaging you.
4. **Test it on one person.** Hand the draft to someone in the audience, or work through it yourself from a blank sheet. Fix every step where they get stuck. Show: a short note of what you changed.
5. **Publish it, then deliver it at the watering hole.** Put the full piece on your own site. In the watering hole, answer the actual question inside the comment itself. Link to the long version only where the thread and the house rules welcome it. Read each community's written and unwritten rules before you post. Show: the live URL and a link to every place you shared it.
6. **Invite readers onto the list.** End the piece with one plain offer: more fixes like this, by email. Show: the signup form on the live page and the list count before and after.
7. **Send it to the list.** Email each new e-bomb to your subscribers. Put the core fix in the email body so it helps even if nobody clicks. Show: the sent email.
8. **Log the response and keep going.** A week after publishing, record replies, signups, shares and reader questions for the piece. Add new questions to the pain log. Publish on a steady rhythm, weekly for most founders. Add each piece to the content wall, which is one page listing every e-bomb by the problem it fixes. Show: a response row per e-bomb and the content wall URL.

## Artifacts produced

- An `ebomb` artifact for each piece: the pain entry it serves, the one-line promise, the live URL and where it was shared.
- A `content_wall` artifact: the URL of the page that indexes every published e-bomb.
- An `email_list` artifact: where signups came from, and the subscriber count by week.
- A `response_log` artifact: replies, signups, shares and reader questions for each piece.
- New pain entries in the pain log, taken from reader questions.

## Anti-patterns

- **The product tour in disguise.** Every step in the post routes through your tool. To spot it, delete every mention of your product. If the reader can no longer fix the problem, you wrote an ad.
- **Topic from your head.** You wrote about what interests you. To spot it, check the pain link: either the post has none, or the linked entry has only one quote.
- **The survey post.** "Everything about month-end close." To spot it, look at the promise. It needs an "and", or a reader can't finish the piece in one sitting.
- **Drive-by linking.** You drop a link into a thread with no answer attached. To spot it, remove the link. If the comment is useless without it, that's the problem. Moderators deleting your posts is the late warning.
- **Counting views.** The founder reports traffic as proof that the content works. To spot it, check the response log. It shows page views but no replies or signups.
- **The silent list.** Subscribers hear nothing until there is something to sell. To spot it, look for a gap of more than a month between sends.

## Gate criteria

The `stage-6-content-and-audience` gate asks for a published content wall and a list that responds. From this card's work, the auditor looks for:

- A live content wall where every e-bomb traces back to a pain entry with quotes.
- Links showing each e-bomb was delivered in at least one watering hole.
- Subscribers who came in through e-bombs, and a response log showing people writing back or sharing, not only opening.
- No product pitch in any e-bomb on the wall.
