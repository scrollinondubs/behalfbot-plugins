# Pick one channel with Bullseye

## Purpose

Find the one place that keeps bringing the right people onto your list. Prove it with numbers from a real test, not a hunch.

## When to use

Use it at stage 6, once a few pieces of content are live and the signup form works. You need somewhere to send people and something worth sending them to.

This is the wrong card if the list is empty because nothing is published yet. What to write belongs to the sibling card 'E-bombs: teach before you sell'. If visitors arrive and leave without signing up, the page is the problem. Go to 'Pain-Dream-Fix: the page that sells'. Growth metrics wait for stage 8.

## Principles

- At this stage one channel usually carries the load. Two channels that half work carry nothing.
- Your gut ranks channels by comfort. The brainstorm is there to overrule it.
- A test is small, short and cheap, and it answers one question. Tuning comes later, and only in the winner.
- Every test gets a pass mark before it starts. A test without one cannot fail, so it teaches nothing.
- Traction gets half your time. Weinberg and Mares suggest splitting the week evenly between building product and getting traction.

## Procedure

1. **Write down what the list has to reach, and by when.** Tie the number to a decision. A founder with an invoice-chasing bot for solo wedding photographers might write: "150 photographers on the list and 25 replies to the welcome email by 15 December, and then I stop DMing people on Instagram and start charging." List only the moves that must happen, in order, for that sentence to come true. Weinberg and Mares call that ordered list the critical path. Anything not on it can wait. *Show: the sentence and the numbered path.*

2. **List ideas for every channel, including the ones you would never pick.** Traction names nineteen channels, and the discipline is filling a row for each one. Take a shift-swap app for independent cafés: a free rota template generator, a stand at a regional coffee expo, a sponsored barista newsletter, thirty walk-ins with a flyer, a page for people searching "cafe staff rota template". Next to each idea, guess three things: what one signup costs, how many people it could reach, and how many days until you would know. *Show: the sheet, one row per idea, guesses filled in.*

3. **Rank into rings.** Bullseye, the framework Weinberg and Mares built, sorts every channel into three rings: promising, possible and long shots. Long shots stay on the sheet for the next round. *Show: every channel placed in a ring.*

4. **Pick the inner ring.** This is usually about three channels. Draw the line where your excitement drops off sharply. Three lets you test in parallel without splitting your attention to nothing. *Show: the chosen channels, with one reason each.*

5. **Test cheaply, with the verdict written first.** Cap each test at about two weeks, a few evenings and $300. Write the number that counts as a pass before anything goes live, for example "30 cafés sign up from the rota generator in 14 days, no ad spend". Give each channel its own UTM link or form. At the end, log cost per signup, whether signups got harder to win as the test went on, and whether the people who arrived match your stage 5 foothold. Replies to the welcome email tell you that fastest. *Show: per test, the pass number, spend, signups and reply rate.*

6. **Choose one and commit.** The channel that beat its pass mark and still has room becomes your channel. Pause the others in writing. If nothing passed, find where each channel broke. Maybe nobody clicked, or people clicked and never signed up. Rerank with that data and run the loop again. *Show: the chosen channel, its numbers, and what you paused.*

## Artifacts produced

- A `traction_goal` artifact: the number, the date and the critical path steps.
- A `channel_brainstorm` artifact: every channel, its ideas, the guessed cost, volume and test length, and its ring.
- One `channel_test` artifact per inner-ring test: the pass mark, dates, spend, signups, replies, and pass or fail.
- A `chosen_channel` artifact: the channel, its test numbers and the channels paused.

## Anti-patterns

- **The favourite channel.** The sheet is full of ideas for the channel you already post in, and the other rows are blank. Spot it when any channel has zero ideas.
- **Grading after the fact.** The results come in and the founder decides afterwards that they look fine. Spot it when a test has no pass mark dated before it started.
- **Tuning instead of testing.** Eight ad variants and three landing pages, still being tweaked in week three. That is scaling work spent on a channel that has not yet earned it.
- **Everything stays on.** A month later four channels are "doing okay". Spot it when nothing has been paused.
- **Counting the wrong people.** A giveaway brings 400 signups and zero replies. Signups who never respond do not make a responsive list.
- **All product, no traction.** The calendar shows features every day and channel work never. Spot it when no critical path step moved this week.

## Gate criteria

The `stage-6-content-and-audience` gate needs a responsive list and a published content wall. From this card, the auditor looks for:

- a test record for each inner-ring channel, with a pass mark set before the test began
- one chosen channel, with signup and reply numbers that trace to the list
- list growth after the choice that comes mostly from that channel, with replies showing the list responds
