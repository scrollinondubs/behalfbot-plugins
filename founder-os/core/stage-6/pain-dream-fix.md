# Pain-Dream-Fix: the page that sells

## Purpose

Write one landing or sales page that readers on your list recognise as being about them. That recognition should turn into a click on the offer.

## When to use

Use this card at stage 6. You need three things first: the one-sentence job from stage 5, a pain log with real quotes in the ledger, and a list that opens your emails.

It is the wrong card if the pain log is thin or mostly your own guesses. Go back to Sales Safari, Amy Hoy and Alex Hillman's method of reading your audience where they already talk, and collect more quotes. If you have no list yet, start with the sibling card 'E-bombs: teach before you sell'. Asking a person for money in a live conversation belongs to stage 7.

## Principles

- The reader should see their own sentence before they see yours. Every pain line comes from a ledger quote.
- A dream is an ordinary day after the problem has gone, measured the way the reader measures it: hours, clients, books closed on time. Features do not count as a dream.
- Describe the after state directly. "Month-end without the spreadsheet" still puts the spreadsheet in the reader's head.
- The product comes last. When the fix appears, the reader should already wish it existed.
- Each paragraph does one job. If it can't be tagged pain, dream or fix, cut it.

## Procedure

Pain-Dream-Fix is Amy Hoy and Alex Hillman's structure for a page that persuades. The example below is a tool that matches Shopify payouts to orders for small e-commerce bookkeepers.

1. **Pull the pain lines.** From the pain log, pick the five to eight quotes closest to your one-sentence job. Rank them by how specific they are. If a quote is vague, like "it's a mess", swap in what this audience means by it, using other quotes as your guide. *Show: a shortlist with a ledger id on every quote.*
2. **Write the pain section.** Open with the sharpest quote as the headline or first line, for example "I lose the first three days of every month matching payouts by hand." Follow it with two or three short paragraphs built from the other quotes. Don't add any claims of your own. *Show: the pain section, each line tied to a ledger id.*
3. **Turn each pain into a dream.** Put the pain in the first person, then write the day on which it never happens: "Books close on the 2nd and every payout already ties to its orders." Use the reader's words and units. *Show: a two-column table of pain lines and dream lines.*
4. **Write the fix and the offer.** Name the product and show in three or four concrete steps how it gets the reader from the pain to the dream. Then state what they get, the price or the ask (early access, a waitlist, a founding plan) and one call to action. *Show: the full page draft.*
5. **Tag every paragraph.** Ask Laya to tag each paragraph as pain, dream or fix. Rewrite anything that has no tag, or that sits in the wrong part of the page. Remove any pain line that has no ledger id. *Show: a tagged draft with no untagged paragraphs and no unsourced pain lines.*
6. **Test on a small segment.** Send the page to 50 to 200 subscribers whose quotes are closest to the pain on the page. Record opens, clicks, replies and signups. Read the replies for phrases they use that you didn't. *Show: a segment test record in the ledger.*
7. **Revise and publish.** Fix the weakest section based on the test. Publish the page and link it from your content wall. *Show: the live URL and the version number.*

## Artifacts produced

- A `sales_page` artifact: URL, version and the paragraph tag map from Laya.
- Pain and dream pairs, each linked to its pain log entry.
- A `segment_test` artifact: segment definition and size, send date, opens, clicks, replies and signups.
- New pain entries taken from test replies, added to the pain log with the reply as their source.

## Anti-patterns

- **Founder-voice pain.** "Reconciliation is an inefficient manual workflow." You can spot it because it has no ledger id and uses a phrase no bookkeeper would type into a forum.
- **Feature dream.** The dream section lists sync, dashboards and an API. You can spot it because the nouns come from your codebase, not from the reader's day.
- **Fix up front.** The product name is in the headline. You can spot it because the reader learns what the thing is before they learn why they would care.
- **Negative dream.** "No more spreadsheets." You can spot it because a pain word shows up inside the dream section.
- **Blended paragraphs.** Laya tags one paragraph as both pain and fix. Split it in two.
- **Whole-list first send.** Version one goes to everyone at once. You can spot it because the ledger has no segment test from before the publish date.

## Gate criteria

The `stage-6-content-and-audience` gate needs a responsive list and a published content wall. From this card, the auditor looks for four things:

- A live sales page linked from the content wall.
- A tag map with every paragraph marked pain, dream or fix.
- A ledger id behind every pain line.
- A segment test record whose numbers show the list responded, through replies, clicks or signups, not just opens.
