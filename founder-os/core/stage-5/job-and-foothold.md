# One sentence for the job, one segment for the foothold

## Purpose

Leave stage 5 with two things. One sentence that says what people hire your product for, using the [[job-to-be-done|Jobs to be Done]] idea from Christensen, Hall, Dillon and Duncan. And one first segment where you can win before any large player bothers to push back.

## When to use

Use it at stage 5. Stage 2 should have produced job clusters, and stage 4 should have test results showing which cluster people paid for, signed up for or switched for.

It is the wrong card if stage 4 left no clear winner. Run another test instead of picking a job by taste. It is also the wrong card for sizing the market or planning expansion. That belongs to the sibling card 'Own a small market before a big one'.

## Principles

- A job sentence starts from a situation and the change someone wants out of it. Who they are comes second.
- Know which door you are walking through. In The Innovator's Solution, Christensen and Raynor split disruptive innovation into low-end disruption, where overshot customers (a bookkeeper paying for a full accounting suite to categorise bank feeds) switch to something good enough and cheaper, and new-market disruption. That second door competes with nonconsumption: the landlord with three flats who logs repairs in text threads because property software is priced for firms with 200 units.
- A [[foothold]] only holds if the big players would rather give that segment up than defend it.
- The list of what you refuse is part of your position. Without it, you have no position.
- Your first segment can be tiny. It is where you start, not the whole market.

## Procedure

1. **Write the job.** Take the stage 2 cluster that your stage 4 test backed hardest. Fill in: "When [circumstance], [person] wants to [progress]." Example: "When a client invoice is 30 days late, a solo designer wants to chase it without sounding desperate." Show: the sentence, plus the test result behind it.

2. **List what they use today.** Write down every current fix for this job: a spreadsheet, a virtual assistant, a saved email template, or nothing. Show: the list, with one line on where each fix falls short.

3. **Pick the fork.** Low-end: people already pay for a tool that does far more than they use, and they would accept less if it cost less. Example: a five-person agency paying enterprise rates for an approval suite when all it needs is one sign-off step. New-market: people have the job but own no product for it, because it costs too much, needs skill, or depends on someone else. Example: freelancers who never chase small invoices because collection agencies won't take sums under $500. Show: the fork you chose, plus three interview quotes or data points that place the segment there.

4. **Test whether incumbents care.** List each serious incumbent. For each one, write down whether they would work to keep your segment or be glad to lose it because of low margins, heavy support or small contracts. If your offer is a better version of what any one incumbent sells to its best accounts, that incumbent will fight. Pick another foothold. Show: a table with three columns: incumbent, would they defend it, and why.

5. **Name the segment.** Describe it by circumstance first and by a group you can actually find second. Then compare it with your stage 1 [[bowling-pin]] segment. The foothold may narrow that pin or replace it. Show: the segment in one line, plus a note on how it relates to the stage 1 pin.

6. **Write what it is not for.** List three to five customers or uses you will turn away for now. Show: the list, with a reason next to each entry.

## Artifacts produced

- `job_statement`: the sentence, the stage 2 cluster it came from, and the stage 4 evidence.
- `foothold`: the segment, the fork (low-end or new-market), the current fixes, and the incumbent table.
- `not_for`: the excluded segments and uses, each with its reason.

The sibling card 'PR/FAQ v2: rewrite the launch from the evidence' uses all three as input.

## Anti-patterns

- **Demographic job.** The sentence opens with "Millennial founders who..." and describes no situation. Rewrite it around the moment the need shows up.
- **Feature as job.** "Wants AI-powered reconciliation." That is your solution. Ask what they are trying to get done when they would reach for it.
- **Nicer version of the leader.** Your pitch is the market leader's product, but friendlier, aimed at its biggest accounts. The leader will defend those accounts, and it usually wins.
- **Nonconsumers who don't care.** Some people use nothing because the job doesn't matter to them. Check that stage 2 found them struggling or hacking together workarounds, not shrugging.
- **Cheap plan that loses money.** The low-end price only works while you subsidise it. A good enough offer that loses money on every account is not a foothold.
- **A not-for list that costs nothing.** It names only customers you never wanted. A real exclusion turns away someone you would have been tempted to serve.

## Gate criteria

The `stage-5-position-and-story` gate needs a one-sentence job, a foothold segment and a PR/FAQ v2. This card supplies the first two. The auditor will check that:

- the job sentence names a circumstance and the progress wanted, and traces back to a stage 2 cluster and a stage 4 result
- the foothold names its fork and its segment, and has an incumbent table showing that no major player would defend the segment
- a not-for list is present

The PR/FAQ v2 comes from its sibling card.
