# A commitment signal costs the customer something

A conversation went well only if the other person gave up something to keep it
going: their time, their reputation or their money.

Time is a follow-up meeting or a trial they set aside hours for. Reputation is an
introduction to their boss or a peer, which they only make if they will not look
foolish. Money is a deposit, a pre-order or a signed pilot with a price. Praise,
"keep me posted" and "I'd pay for that" cost nothing, so they tell you nothing
about what the person will do. Talk about paying is not a payment.

Ending each conversation with a concrete ask is how a founder finds out which
kind of interest they actually have.

Credit: Rob Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test, which frames commitment as time,
reputation and money. Read the original: https://www.momtestbook.com
