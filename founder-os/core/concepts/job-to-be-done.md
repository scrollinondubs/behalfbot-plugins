# People hire a product to make progress in a situation

A job to be done is the progress a person is trying to make in a particular
circumstance. Customers do not buy products; they hire them for a job, and fire
them when something does the job better.

Stating the job as a situation plus the progress wanted ("when a client's
payment is two weeks late and payroll is Friday, I want to get paid without
souring the relationship") keeps it free of any product. It also shows the real
competition: whatever the person hires today, including a spreadsheet, a
favour from a friend, or doing nothing at all. Every job has a functional side
and social and emotional sides too, and the last two often decide the hire.

Credit: Clayton Christensen and colleagues, "Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be
Done" (HBR, 2016), and the Christensen Institute. Read the original:
https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done
