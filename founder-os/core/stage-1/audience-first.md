# Audience first, product second

## Purpose

Pick a group of real people you can reach and learn from, and describe them by the work they do and what keeps them up at night. Their needs decide what you build. Your idea doesn't get a vote yet.

## When to use

Start of stage 1, before you look for watering holes. It matters most if you already have an app, built or half-built, and your real question is "who do I sell this to?"

Wrong card if you can already name your audience and back it with evidence. Go to *Watering holes: find where they already talk* and *First bowling-pin segment*. Also wrong if you have no problem area at all. That's stage 0.

## Principles

- In 30x500, Amy Hoy and Alex Hillman call this audience first. The reason it works is practical. A group of people is something you can go and watch this week. An idea isn't.
- Describe behaviour. Name their role, the job they are trying to get done and what they already spend on it. Industry labels and headcounts tell you nothing you can act on.
- Start from the inside. Pick people you have been, have served or have worked next to. If you have to learn their world from reports, every step after this one is slower.
- A group that pays for tools, templates or help with its work will pay again. A group that pays for nothing is a hard first audience.
- The pick is a guess. Research might kill it in a few hours. That's cheap, and it still counts as progress.

## Procedure

1. **Park the app.** Write one paragraph on what you built and who you imagined using it. Label it "parked". *Show:* the parked paragraph.
2. **List candidates from your own life.** Go through jobs you have held, clients you have worked for and communities you already belong to. Aim for five to ten groups. *Show:* the list, with your connection to each group: member, served, or close observer.
3. **Rewrite each candidate as a behaviour line.** Use the pattern: role, recurring job, something they already buy for that job. Example: "Freelance video editors running revision rounds for three to eight clients at once, who already pay for Frame.io and extra cloud storage." Cut or rewrite any line that still reads like an industry or an age bracket. *Show:* the rewritten lines.
4. **Score each line.** Ask three questions. Can you name five actual people in this group? Do they talk about their work in public online? Do they pay to make that work easier? Mark each answer yes, no or unknown. *Show:* the scoring table.
5. **Pick one.** The winner is usually the row with the most yes marks and the fewest unknowns. Commit it to one line, then make the case against your old target in two sentences. A founder whose booking app was aimed at "small businesses" might write: "I already know a dozen solo physiotherapists who lose Friday afternoons to rescheduling. I cannot name a single small business owner who would take my call." *Show:* the chosen line and the two-sentence case.
6. **Hand it off.** Save the audience line to the ledger. Finding where these people talk and picking a first segment inside the group each have their own card. *Show:* the saved `audience` artifact.

What the switch looks like: a founder built a client-portal tool "for agencies". They spent four years as a freelance video editor. Rewritten, their audience became editors who keep losing track of client feedback across email, Slack and review links. They could name twenty of those editors. They could not name one "agency" buyer.

## Artifacts produced

- An `artifacts` row of kind `audience`: the chosen behaviour line, your connection to the group, and the scoring table for every candidate.
- An `artifacts` row of kind `parked_idea`: the original app paragraph, kept so you can come back to it later with evidence in hand.

## Anti-patterns

- **Idea looking for a home.** You chose the audience by asking who might want the app you already have. Tell: the audience line describes product features instead of people's work.
- **Market as audience.** "SMBs", "healthcare", "the creator economy". Tell: you can't name five members, or when the description is a dollar figure.
- **Demographic as audience.** "Millennial parents", "Gen Z". The members share an age or life stage but no common job. Tell: no behaviour line fits the whole group.
- **Tourist pick.** You chose a group you have never worked with because the space looks hot. Tell: all their vocabulary comes from analyst reports and none comes from conversations.
- **Stuck comparing.** Weeks go by weighing candidates and none gets tested. Tell: there is still no single audience line after a week at this stage.

## Gate criteria

The `stage-1-audience` gate asks for three things: a named audience, at least three active watering holes, and a first bowling-pin segment. This card supplies the named audience. That means one behaviour line naming role, recurring job and current spending, backed by a scoring table in which you can name five real members.

The gate fails if the audience is a market category or a demographic, or if the line was written to fit an existing app. The watering holes and the segment come from the sibling cards.
