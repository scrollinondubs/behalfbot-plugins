# What angels screen for

## Purpose

This card turns a loose list of possible backers into a short one you brief every month. The target is 8 to 12 people who have already funded tools for the kind of customer you sell to. If you run a payroll add-on for dental groups, that means people who have backed software for practice owners.

Start sending them progress well before any round opens. When you finally ask, they have seen you set monthly goals and watched how many you actually hit.

## When to use

Use this card at stage 9, once stages 7 and 8 have produced paying customers and a clear use for outside money. If you have not yet decided whether to raise, work through the sibling card 'Should you raise at all?' first. If meetings are already booked and you need the deck, use 'Pitch in the order investors believe' instead.

## Principles

- An angel writes a personal cheque while the company is too young to value, so the bet is mostly on you.
- Traction you paid for yourself is the strongest evidence of grit you have. A solo founder who shipped three releases of a shift-rota app on evenings and savings, with no cheque in sight, has already answered what an angel is trying to predict.
- Every number has to survive a follow-up question. A customer is someone who pays, counted over a stated period.
- The market case explains why the timing works now. A large figure from an analyst report does not.
- Regular updates build trust before any ask. Angels like to watch you hit your own targets a few times before they commit.
- Calacanis covers syndicates: followers who invest on one lead's terms.

## Procedure

1. **Pull the proof from the ledger.** List what you shipped in the last ninety days, your paying accounts, MRR by month and churn. Show: a one-page progress sheet with dates.
2. **Test every number.** Write the definition next to each metric and ask yourself the follow-up an angel would ask. Say a scheduling tool for physio clinics claims "40 customers". The honest version might be 40 signups, 11 paying and 9 still paying after two months. Show: the sheet with definitions and the honest figures.
3. **Write the market case in five lines.** Who buys, what they use today, what changed recently that makes your approach workable, why earlier attempts stalled, and what you would own if you win. Show: the market note.
4. **Start wide.** Aim for 20 to 30 names. If you sell a Stripe reconciliation tool to bookkeepers, that means people who funded accounting software, tools for small agencies, or anything billed per seat to finance teams. Mine portfolio pages, founders in your network who closed a round last year, and syndicate leads on AngelList. Show: a sheet listing each person, the source of the lead and the deals that make them relevant.
5. **Score fit and cut.** For each name, ask four things. Have they backed this kind of buyer before? Can they introduce you to customers or later investors? Do they know the domain well enough to save you an expensive mistake? Does their usual cheque match your round? Keep 8 to 12. Show: the short list with a one-line fit note for each name.
6. **Flag anyone who brings a group.** One yes from certain names pulls in other people's money too. For a $250k round on a marketplace booking freelance sound engineers for wedding venues, a lead whose group usually adds $80k covers a third of it. Check each name's AngelList page, and put those people first on your update list. Show: the flagged rows with a typical group size.
7. **Write the first update from the ledger.** Include the headline metric against last month, what shipped, what broke, the one problem you are stuck on, and one ask that is not money (for example, an intro to the operations lead at a clinic chain). Keep it to one screen. Show: the update, saved to the ledger.
8. **Fix the schedule and never skip it.** Anyone joining the list learns the rhythm up front, for example the second Tuesday of every month, along with the rough month you expect to open a round. Keeping to the schedule is the signal angels read. If your largest marketplace seller leaves in March, the March update still goes out on the second Tuesday and says so plainly. Show: a send log with dates and replies.

## Artifacts produced

- A `progress_sheet` artifact: shipped work, paying accounts, MRR and churn, each with its definition.
- A `market_note` artifact: the five-line case for why now.
- An `angel_shortlist` artifact: 8 to 12 names, each with a fit note, a cheque range and a syndicate flag.
- An `investor_update` artifact: the written update, plus a send log.

## Anti-patterns

- **The inflated customer count.** Signups are reported as customers. You can spot it when the number shrinks as soon as someone asks "paying, this month?"
- **Building only after the cheque.** The plan starts once the money lands. You can spot it when nothing has shipped in weeks and the ledger has been quiet since stage 8.
- **The spray list.** Two hundred names copied from a directory. You can spot it when a name's fit note can only say "invests in startups".
- **The update that appears with the ask.** You can spot it when the first message an angel ever gets from you contains round terms.
- **Good news only.** Churn disappears, or the headline metric changes each month to whichever one looks best. Angels who notice stop reading.
- **Long answers to short questions.** An angel asks something in one line and gets back a page. Reread your reply: each answer should fit in a sentence.
- **Spending on the look of a company.** You can spot it when a coworking lease or conference tickets come before revenue covers them.

## Gate criteria

The `stage-9-fundraise` gate opens only if stages 7 and 8 justify a raise. From this card, it looks for:

- ledger evidence from stages 7 and 8 that supports raising money
- a progress sheet whose defined metrics match the ledger
- a short list of 8 to 12 angels, each with a fit note
- at least one investor update drawn from the ledger, sent, with its date

If the stage 7 and 8 evidence does not support a raise, the gate fails however strong the list is.
