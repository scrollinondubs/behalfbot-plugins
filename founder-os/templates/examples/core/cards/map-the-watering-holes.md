# Map the watering holes

> Illustrative example for the authoring templates. It is not shipped to
> founders and it is not a summary of any book.

## Purpose

Turn "my audience is small business owners" into a named group of people you can
go and read today, in the places where they already talk to each other.

## When to use

Use it at stage 1, once the founder has a problem they care about and a PR/FAQ
v0. It is the wrong card if the founder cannot yet say who has the problem. Send
them back to stage 0 instead of guessing an audience for them.

## Principles

- An audience is people, not a market size. You should be able to name ten of
  them.
- They already gather somewhere. See [[watering-hole]].
- You earn the right to sell by listening first. See [[audience-before-product]].
- Activity beats size. A forum with 200 posts a week beats one with 50,000
  silent members.

## Procedure

1. Write down the audience in one line: who they are and what they are trying to
   get done.
2. List every place they might talk shop: forums, subreddits, Slack and Discord
   communities, newsletters with comment sections, trade events, review sites.
3. Visit each one. Record posts per week, whether members answer each other, and
   whether the talk is about the job itself or about something else.
4. Keep the three to five that are active and on topic. Drop the rest.
5. Inside those, pick the narrowest group with the sharpest need. That is the
   first bowling pin to knock over.

## Artifacts produced

- An `audience` artifact: the one-line audience and the first bowling-pin
  segment.
- A `watering_holes` artifact: each place, its URL, activity notes, and keep or
  drop.

## Anti-patterns

- **The whole market.** "Everyone who runs a business" has no watering hole.
  Narrow until one appears.
- **Counting members.** Big and silent is worth less than small and busy.
- **Posting before reading.** The founder announces the product in week one and
  gets ignored or banned.
- **Only where you already hang out.** Your own feed is a sample of one.

## Gate criteria

The `stage-1-audience` gate looks for a named audience, at least three watering
holes marked active with evidence, and a first bowling-pin segment narrower than
the audience.
