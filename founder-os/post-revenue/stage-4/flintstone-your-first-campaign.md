## What this is
Fred Flintstone's car had no engine. He ran it with his feet. Flintstoning means doing the thing by hand the first time, so you learn whether it works before you build the machine. Sean wrote about it on Grid7, and the post went viral for a reason: **automate only once you have made it work yourself.**

For cold outreach, that means no tools on the first run. You find people the hard way, look them up, write to each one, and see what comes back.

## What you make
A hand-run campaign to at least 20 people who look like your best customers. You pick them one by one, write a short personal message to each, and track who replied and what they said.

## How to submit
Write up who you targeted and why, your message (one example is enough), how many you sent, how many replied, and what the replies taught you.

## Done when
- You contacted at least 20 real people, each chosen on purpose, not from a bought list.
- Each message was personal enough that it could not be sent to anyone else unchanged.
- You recorded sends, replies and outcomes.
- You wrote what you would change before running it again, based on the replies.

## Coach checks
- The point is learning, not volume. Push back if they reached for a tool or a list before the hand run.
- Messages that lead with the product and a meeting request get a note. Lead with them, their problem, one short ask.
- Respect the law and the recipient: in the EU, B2B outreach needs a legitimate interest and an easy way to opt out. Personal addresses and consumers are off limits for cold email.
- A low reply rate is still a pass if they learned from it and said what.
