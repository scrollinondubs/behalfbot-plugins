## What this is
You have a funnel map with numbers on it. Now the hard part: deciding which leak to work on first. The options usually boil down to three. Get more people into the top of the hose (traffic). Get more of the people already there to the next step (conversion). Or make the product itself better, so the people who try it stay and tell others.

Each of our books comes at this from a different angle. Traction says focus on the one channel that can move the needle and test it cheaply. Lean Analytics says find the stage your business is in and watch the metric that matters for that stage. The Startup Owner's Manual reminds you that pouring traffic into a product people do not keep is just a faster way to burn money.

**Fix the leak closest to the money that you can actually move.** If retention is broken, more traffic will not save you.

## What you make
A short written decision: the leak you will work on for the next few weeks, the number you expect to move, and the reasoning. Include the one option you considered and rejected, and why.

## How to submit
Pick the focus that fits, then write your decision and reasoning in the box.

## Done when
- You named one leak, tied to a specific step and number on your funnel map.
- You said what you expect to change and by roughly how much.
- You wrote down the option you rejected and why.
- If retention or activation is weak, you explained why traffic is still the right call, or you chose the product.

## Coach checks
- The classic mistake is choosing traffic because it feels like progress, while the numbers show people leave after one use. Ask what happens to last month's new customers.
- "Better product" is a valid answer. Do not treat it as giving up on marketing.
- The decision should be falsifiable: a number, a step, a time frame.
- It is fine for the call to be wrong. The point is to make it on purpose and check it later.
- Do not require them to read all three books. The card is the commercial; the books are for those who want depth.
