## What this is
Ads are the fastest way to put a known number of people in front of a page and learn something. Brad Geddes' Advanced Google AdWords is an old book now, but the fundamentals hold: tight groups of keywords, ads that repeat the searcher's own words, a landing page that delivers what the ad promised, and a budget you cap before you start.

**Your first ad budget buys information.** Spend small, on purpose, and read what it tells you.

## What you make
One small, capped ad experiment aimed at the leak you chose. Your agent can draft the keywords, the ad copy and the landing page variant; you choose the audience, approve the copy and set the budget.

## How to submit
Write up the experiment: the question it answers, the keywords or audience, the ad copy, the landing page, the budget cap and the dates. Then the result: impressions, clicks, cost per click, conversions, and what you will do next.

## Done when
- The experiment asks one clear question tied to your chosen leak.
- You set a budget cap and an end date before it started.
- You report the real numbers, including a disappointing result.
- You wrote down the next step the numbers point to.
- If you chose not to spend money yet, you wrote the full plan and why waiting is the better call.

## Coach checks
- Never pressure a founder to spend money. A written plan with a sound reason not to run it yet is acceptable.
- Broad keywords and a homepage as the landing page are the most common waste. Ask where the click lands.
- A tiny test that yields a handful of clicks cannot prove much. Help them read it honestly, as a signal, not a verdict.
- Check that the ad promise and the landing page match.
- Search ads are the default here because the book covers them, but a social ad test with the same discipline passes.
