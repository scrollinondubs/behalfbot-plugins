## What this is
Ryan Chapman was an Infusionsoft guy too, and his book How To Fix Your Funnel starts where you should: figure out the lay of the land. Before you change anything, map what you do today. Where do people land? What do they see next? What do you send them? Where does each step hand them to the next one, and where does it drop them?

**You cannot fix a funnel you have never drawn.** Most founders discover steps they forgot existed, and gaps where they assumed something was happening.

## What you make
A diagram of your current funnel, from the places people first find you through to a repeat purchase or referral. Each box is a real page, email, ad or conversation. Each arrow gets the number of people who made it across last month, from the dashboard you just built.

## How to submit
Share a link to the diagram (a drawing tool, a photo of paper, or a diagram your agent generated), and write two or three sentences on what surprised you.

## Done when
- Every entry point you know about is on the map, including word of mouth and anything manual.
- Each step is a real thing someone sees or receives, not a stage name.
- The arrows carry numbers from your analytics, or are marked unknown.
- You circled the two or three choke points where the biggest share of people drop off.

## Coach checks
- A map that copies the lifecycle phases word for word has missed the point. Ask for the actual pages and emails.
- Manual steps (a founder replying to emails by hand, a sales call) belong on the map. They are often the real funnel.
- Choke points should come from the numbers, not from instinct. If the numbers are unknown, the choke point call is a guess and should say so.
- Do not let them start fixing anything here. Deciding where to focus is the next stage.
