## What this is
Lifecycle marketing is an idea from the Infusionsoft days (the company is now called Keap). Sean was an Infusionsoft consultant, and it shaped how he thinks about every business since. The idea: people move along a continuum of increasing engagement. You attract strangers to your site, capture their contact details so they become leads, nurture those leads, convert them into customers, deliver more than they expected, then upsell them and turn them into people who refer others.

**Every business already has these phases, whether or not anyone designed them.** This card is about seeing yours.

## What you make
A one-page map of your own lifecycle. For each phase (attract, capture, nurture, convert, deliver and exceed, upsell, refer), write what happens today in one or two lines, and roughly how many people are in that phase right now. Where nothing happens, say so. Blank phases are the most useful thing on the page.

## How to submit
Paste your map in the box. A simple list, one phase per line, is fine.

## Done when
- All seven phases are there, each with what actually happens today, not what you wish happened.
- Each phase has a rough count of people, or says plainly that you do not know yet.
- You marked the one phase you think is weakest and said why in a sentence.

## Coach checks
- Push back on aspirational answers. "We send a welcome sequence" when it was never set up is the most common miss. Ask what the last person to sign up actually received.
- Unknown counts are fine at this card. They become the to-do list for the analytics card that follows.
- The "deliver and exceed" phase is often skipped because it feels like operations, not marketing. Ask what a new customer experiences in their first week.
- Do not let them redesign the whole lifecycle here. The job is an honest snapshot.
- A founder whose referrals phase is empty is normal at this stage. Note it; viral loops come in stage 4 of this track.
