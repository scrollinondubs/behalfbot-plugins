## What this is
This is Sean's own framing. An advisory board is a small group of people who have done what you are trying to do, and who agree to give you a little time on a regular rhythm. Not investors, not employees. People whose judgment fills the gaps in yours.

**Pick advisors for the gaps on your canvas, not for the names on their résumé.** One person who has scaled a sales team is worth more to a founder about to hire sales than three famous names who will never reply.

## What you make
A plan for your board: the two or three gaps it should cover, the people you will ask, what you ask of them (time, rhythm, format), and what they get in return. Then ask at least one.

## How to submit
Write up the gaps, the names and why each fits, the ask and the offer, and how the first conversations went.

## Done when
- Each seat on the board maps to a specific gap in your business.
- The ask is concrete: how much time, how often, in what format.
- You said what advisors get (equity, cash, access, or simply the satisfaction) and kept it fair to both sides.
- You asked at least one person, or wrote why you are not forming a board yet.

## Coach checks
- A board of friends who will only agree with them is the common failure. Ask what each person will disagree with them about.
- Equity for advisors should be small and vest over time. Flag anything large or immediate.
- Choosing not to form a board yet is a valid outcome if the reasoning is sound.
