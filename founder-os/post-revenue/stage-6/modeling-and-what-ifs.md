## What this is
This comes from Sean's own experience. A good model of your business is a set of connected numbers you can poke: traffic, conversion at each step, price, churn, costs. Change one and see what happens to revenue and cash six and twelve months out.

**Think of the model as a question machine.** What if conversion doubled? What if you raised prices 20 percent and lost a tenth of new sign-ups? What does it take to hire your first person?

## What you make
A model built from your real funnel numbers, with the inputs on one side and revenue, costs and cash over time on the other, plus three what-if scenarios you ran and what they told you. Your agent builds the spreadsheet or app; you choose the questions.

## How to submit
Share a link to the model, and write up your three scenarios: what you changed, what happened, and what you will do about it.

## Done when
- The inputs come from your actual analytics, not industry averages.
- Changing an input updates the outputs, so you can run a scenario in a minute.
- You ran three what-if scenarios and wrote one decision or question each one raised.
- You marked which assumption you are least sure of.

## Coach checks
- Models built from benchmark numbers fail. Ask where each input came from.
- Hockey-stick outputs are a smell. Ask which input is doing the work.
- The decision from each scenario matters more than the polish of the model.
- A simple spreadsheet passes. So does a small app.
