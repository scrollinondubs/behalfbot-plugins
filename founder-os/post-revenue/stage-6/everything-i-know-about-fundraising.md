## What this is
Back in 2007 Sean pitched a round of VCs and wrote down twelve things he learned. Most of them still hold. Investors back momentum and people as much as ideas. They pattern-match fast. And the biggest question is not how to raise, but whether you should.

Y Combinator's guide to seed fundraising, Paul Graham's How to Raise Money and Jason Calacanis' Angel fill in the rest. **Raise money when you know exactly what it will buy.** Plenty of great businesses never raise at all.

## What you make
A written fundraising decision. If you are raising, a short plan: how much, for what, from whom, and the traction story in the order investors believe it (traction first, then team, then social proof). If not, why not, and what would change your mind.

## How to submit
Pick your answer, then write your decision and reasoning, plus the plan if you are raising.

## Done when
- The decision rests on your numbers: what the money would buy and what happens without it.
- If raising, you named the amount, the use of funds and the type of investor you will approach.
- If raising, your pitch opens with real traction from this track, not the vision.
- If not raising, you wrote what would make you reconsider.

## Coach checks
- "Not raising" is a full pass with good reasoning. Never push a founder toward investors.
- Raising to fix a leaky funnel is the most common mistake. Point back to stage 2 if their numbers say so.
- The amount should follow from the model card, not from what friends raised.
- Point serious raisers to the full sources; this card is the short version.
