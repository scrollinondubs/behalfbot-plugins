## What this is
In lifecycle marketing, a lead magnet is the thing you give away in exchange for someone's contact details: a checklist, a template, a short guide, a free tool. It turns an anonymous visitor into a lead you can nurture. The follow-up sequence is what happens next: a few emails that help, build trust, and make the next step to buying feel natural.

**The best lead magnet solves one small problem completely.** Not an ebook about everything. One useful thing your customers would happily pay a little for.

## What you make
One lead magnet aimed at the people at your weakest step, a page or form that offers it, and a follow-up sequence of three to five emails. Your agent drafts the asset and the emails from what you know about your customers; you sharpen them until they sound like you.

## How to submit
Share the link to the page with the offer, then paste the subject line and a one-line summary of each follow-up email.

## Done when
- The lead magnet solves one specific problem your customers have, in their words.
- The page is live and you signed up with a test address and received the magnet and the first email.
- The sequence has three to five emails, each with one clear job, and the last makes a clear offer.
- You know how you will measure it: sign-up rate on the page and sales from the sequence.

## Coach checks
- Generic magnets ("our newsletter", "a free consultation") fail. Ask which customer problem it solves in one sentence.
- The test sign-up is required. Broken delivery is common and silent.
- The sequence should help first and sell last. A sequence that pitches in every email gets a note.
- Customer language beats founder language. If they have support emails or reviews, point them there for wording.
