## What this is
If you came through Pre-revenue, you already know Sales Safari and e-bombs from 30x500: read where your audience talks, collect their pains in their words, and publish things that help with them. That habit does not stop after the first sale. It is your research engine and your content engine.

At Pagely, Sean built a system called Leviathan that automated most of the follow-up in a B2B sales process, so prospects got the right message at the right time without a human typing it. This card applies the same thinking to your research and content. **Your agent watches the watering holes, drafts the e-bombs and the social posts; you pick the pains and approve what ships.**

## What you make
A pipeline that runs every week: your agent gathers new pain quotes from your watering holes, groups them, drafts one e-bomb and a handful of social posts from them, and puts them in front of you for review before anything goes out.

## How to submit
Describe the pipeline in steps, say what runs on its own and what you approve, and share a link to one e-bomb it produced that you published.

## Done when
- The pipeline runs on a schedule without you starting it.
- A human (you) approves every public post before it goes out.
- It produced at least one published e-bomb and its social posts from real quotes.
- You can trace that e-bomb back to the quotes that inspired it.

## Coach checks
- Fully automated publishing fails. Approval before anything public is the rule.
- Respect the watering holes: reading public posts is fine, scraping behind logins or breaking a forum's rules is not.
- E-bombs must still help first. Drafts that pitch the product in every paragraph get a note.
- Point Pre-revenue graduates back to their own Safari notes rather than starting over.
