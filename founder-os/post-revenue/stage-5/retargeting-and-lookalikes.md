## What this is
Two techniques that make ads much more efficient once you have traffic and customers. Retargeting shows ads to people who already visited your site but did not take the next step. Lookalike audiences ask the ad platform to find new people who resemble your best customers. Brad Geddes covers both in Advanced Google AdWords, and the same ideas apply on every major ad platform.

**Retargeting turns your past visitors into a second chance. Lookalikes turn your customer list into a map.**

## What you make
One retargeting campaign aimed at visitors who stalled at your weakest step, and one lookalike campaign seeded from your best customers. Capped budgets and end dates, set in advance. Your agent sets up the audiences and drafts the ads; you approve the spend and the copy.

## How to submit
Describe both campaigns: the audience, the message, the budget cap and dates, and the results for each against your earlier ad experiment.

## Done when
- Your site's privacy notice and consent banner cover the tracking that retargeting needs.
- The retargeting message speaks to where the person stalled, not a generic pitch.
- The lookalike is seeded from your best customers, not everyone who ever signed up.
- You report real numbers for both and compare them with your earlier ad test.
- If you chose not to spend yet, you wrote the full plan and why waiting is the better call.

## Coach checks
- Never pressure a founder to spend money. A complete plan with a sound reason not to run it yet passes.
- Consent is required in the EU before retargeting pixels fire. Check that it is in place.
- Small audiences may be too small for the platform to serve. Help them read a low-delivery result honestly.
- Frequency caps matter. Ads that follow a visitor everywhere for weeks hurt the brand.
