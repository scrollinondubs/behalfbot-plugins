## What this is
Every pitch has to pass your own limits first: can you build it, launch it and keep it running with the skills, time and money you have today, will it fit your life and feel satisfying, and does it do no harm. Then your customer's: is the format familiar to them, do they already buy things like it at prices like it, and do they believe something like it can help. We run each of your pitches through these checks for you and say which survive and why. You decide: agree, or argue with any call we got wrong. What survives should be tiny, and your pitch lines are enough for a "coming soon" page with a signup box.

## What you make
Your verdict on our analysis, one or two survivors, and a live "coming soon" page for the winner with a signup box.

## How to submit
Press "Analyse my pitches" to get our take on every pitch. Change any call you disagree with and say why (at least one change or comment), pick the winner's format, and share the link to your page. If you pick Other, say what it is in the box.

## Done when
- Every pitch has a pass or fail for your limits and your customer's, each with a one-line reason, and you have checked them.
- Where you disagreed with our analysis, you said why.
- One or two survivors remain, and the winner can be made in under 90 days with skills you already have.
- The winner's format is one your audience already pays for, shown by a buy or recommendation in your notes.
- Your sign-up page is live, built from your pain, dream and fix lines, with a working signup box and no price yet.

## Coach checks
- The first analysis came from us. Check the student engaged with it: any call they changed has a reason grounded in their notes, not just a preference.
- Your own limits first: a pitch that needs new skills, staff or a year of building is out for now, however exciting (pp 180-181).
- The course's cautionary tale: a product that works and sells but demands round-the-clock uptime they could not live with (pp 183-184).
- Selling to a culture that does not buy this way (the course's salon software and spreadsheet-killer examples) fails the customer check (p 186).
- No complaints about a format is not a no. If they pay for similar things it may pass (p 186).
- The course's tiny first products: a book written in a day, a 50-page PDF, a white paper that started a software company (pp 181-182).
- If they pick Other, ask what the format is and check it against the same rules.
