## What this is
Look at what you have done so far. You gathered the facts on Sales Safari, found the insight when you turned a pain into a dream, and picked one pitch with the plinko. Nacho Bassino calls those the steps of a product strategy. His last step is to put it all on one page, and one of the tools he uses for that is Richard Rumelt's kernel. It has three parts:

- Diagnosis: the one problem you are solving, in your audience's words.
- Guiding policy: how you will solve it, and what you will say no to.
- Coherent actions: the three to five parts of the product, and nothing else.

Bassino also asks which assumptions have to be true for the plan to work. Add the one that would sink you if it is wrong, and the number that will tell you.

**This page is your scope control.** When a shiny feature shows up during the build, check it against the diagnosis. If it does not fix that problem, it waits.

## What you make
One page: the diagnosis, the guiding policy, three to five parts, and your riskiest assumption with its number.

## How to submit
Write it yourself in the box, or press "Draft V1 for me" for a first version built from your Safari notes, your dream line, your pitches and your plinko winner. If you use the draft, reshape it by asking for changes or edit it yourself (at least once). Press "Analyse this" any time for feedback against the checklist below, then submit.

## Done when
- The diagnosis quotes your Safari notes.
- The guiding policy has at least two "we will not" lines.
- Every part traces back to the diagnosis. Anything else is cut.
- The assumption has a number you can check at launch, like sign-ups or pre-orders.

## Coach checks
- If this card started from a draft we wrote, check the final is in the student's voice and grounded in their own notes, and that their edits moved it closer to Done when. If they wrote it themselves, judge it on its own.
- Rumelt's main warning: a goal is not a strategy. "Become the go-to tool for X" is a wish. The diagnosis has to name a problem, and the policy has to say how.
- Watch for fluff: big words that say nothing, like "seamless" or "empower". Ask what they mean in plain words.
- The "we will not" lines should cost something. "We will not be evil" is free; "we will not support teams, only solo bookkeepers" is a real choice.
- The parts must fit the plinko winner. If the page describes a bigger product than the one that survived, send it back.
- The rest of Bassino's book (roadmaps, OKRs) is for product teams at scale. Do not ask for any of it here.
- One page is a FounderOS rule. It gives the backwards plan in the next stage a clean input, and the diagnosis becomes the pain line on the sales page.
