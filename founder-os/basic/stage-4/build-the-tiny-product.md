## What this is
Describe the finished thing clearly, work backwards to a list of tiny next steps, then cut the list until it fits. Work in blocks of three or four hours at most, and leave yourself a note for next time. Treat the deadline as a challenge, not a whip, and cut whatever you must to ship.

## What you make
Your backwards plan, a deadline, and the finished product.

## How to submit
Upload the product or share a link to it, and write your plan in the box.

## Done when
- Your plan describes the finished product in one paragraph and breaks it into tasks small enough for one session.
- Your deadline is no more than 90 days after you finished the previous stage.
- The product is done: a stranger could receive it and use it.
- You wrote down what you cut to ship.
- Your plan for the week after launch is already written.

## Coach checks
- Scope creep: a "small guide" that grew chapters. The course's first products took from a day to 50 pages (pp 181-182).
- A good session is one medium task and two small ones (pp 200-201).
- Self-punishment or reward games around the deadline. The course wants "I choose to" and a calm view of done or not done (pp 196-197, 206).
- The four product killers: doing nothing, not finishing, not following a marketing plan, and everything else (p 195). Tech worries and competition belong in everything else.
- The course would not set a deadline more than 90 days out, and a first product should never take nine to twelve months (pp 180, 203).
- The backwards plan starts from one sale: a customer who is ready, a way to reach them, and an offer (pp 127-129).
