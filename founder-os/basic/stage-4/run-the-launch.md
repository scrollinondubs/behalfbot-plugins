## What this is
A launch is a dinner party: a date and time, an RSVP deadline, reminders, and an end. Send three to seven emails that each help on their own and build toward a yes or no, then open the doors with a clear closing time. Decide ahead how many sales would make it worth it, and afterwards celebrate, look back honestly, and go back to Safari and e-bombs.

Jeff Walker's launch steps fit inside those three to seven emails. Start with a sneak peek: tell your list something is coming and ask what two questions they have about the topic. Then send three useful pieces a few days apart. The opportunity: the pain, the fix you found, and how you found it. The transformation: what changes for them, with a real example and one small part they can try now. Ownership: what it is like to have the fix, and a note that an offer is coming. Then open the doors, and close them at the time you said. Inside that, follow Russell Brunson's order: your story first, then the buyer's doubts one at a time (about the method, about themselves, about what is in their way), then the offer. A live webinar is one way to run that order. Emails work just as well. Leave out the pushy parts: no fake countdowns, no bonuses that vanish, no made-up value. A real closing time that you keep is enough.

## What you make
A launch calendar, three to seven launch emails, your doors-open and reminder emails, a results note, and a plan for the next four weeks.

## How to submit
Write your launch emails yourself, or press "Draft V1 for me" for a first set built from your e-bombs, your sales page and your audience's words. If you use the draft, reshape it or edit it yourself (at least once). Press "Analyse this" any time for feedback. After the launch, upload your sent emails or share links to them, and write your results note in the box.

## Done when
- You wrote down your "worth it" number of sales before the doors opened.
- Three to seven launch emails went out, each useful on its own, then a doors-open email with a closing time and at least one reminder before the close.
- At least one person you do not know bought.
- Your results note records sales, list size, and one thing to do better next launch.
- The next four weeks of Safari sessions and e-bombs are on your calendar.

## Coach checks
- If this card started from a draft we wrote, check the final is in the student's voice and grounded in their own notes, and that their edits moved it closer to Done when. If they wrote it themselves, judge it on its own.
- The course's launch template: one email reframes the main pain, one gives a tiny win in under 30 minutes, one a bigger win, one is all dream, one is a sample the day before the doors open (pp 214-215).
- "I'm proud to announce" with no build-up is the course's own one-sale flop (pp 211, 224-225).
- No explicit ask for the sale. People need to be shown the door (p 226).
- No deadline, so the tab stays open forever (pp 212-213).
- A list of 150 to 200 is plenty and 100 is fine (pp 209-210). The course's own worth-it example was 50 sales (p 231).
- After launch, dropping Safari and asking customers "would you buy this" instead (p 234).
- Post-launch slump: no next plan written (pp 205, 228-229). Debrief against the flop checklist whether it flopped or not (pp 225-227).
- Walker's own page leans on scarcity: vanishing bonuses, price rises and several emails on the last day. FounderOS keeps his order and drops the pressure. Flag any deadline, bonus or price change that is not real.
- One sale to a stranger is a FounderOS number. Sales beyond that are their own worth-it number, not ours.
