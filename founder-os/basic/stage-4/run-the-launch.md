## What this is
A launch is a dinner party: a date and time, an RSVP deadline, reminders, and an end. Send three to seven emails that each help on their own and build toward a yes or no, then open the doors with a clear closing time. Decide ahead how many sales would make it worth it, and afterwards celebrate, look back honestly, and go back to Safari and e-bombs.

## What you make
A launch calendar, three to seven launch emails, your doors-open and reminder emails, a results note, and a plan for the next four weeks.

## How to submit
Upload your emails or share links to them, and write your results note in the box.

## Done when
- You wrote down your "worth it" number of sales before the doors opened.
- Three to seven launch emails went out, each useful on its own, then a doors-open email with a closing time and at least one reminder before the close.
- At least one person you do not know bought.
- Your results note records sales, list size, and one thing to do better next launch.
- The next four weeks of Safari sessions and e-bombs are on your calendar.

## Coach checks
- The course's launch template: one email reframes the main pain, one gives a tiny win in under 30 minutes, one a bigger win, one is all dream, one is a sample the day before the doors open (pp 214-215).
- "I'm proud to announce" with no build-up is the course's own one-sale flop (pp 211, 224-225).
- No explicit ask for the sale. People need to be shown the door (p 226).
- No deadline, so the tab stays open forever (pp 212-213).
- A list of 150 to 200 is plenty and 100 is fine (pp 209-210). The course's own worth-it example was 50 sales (p 231).
- After launch, dropping Safari and asking customers "would you buy this" instead (p 234).
- Post-launch slump: no next plan written (pp 205, 228-229). Debrief against the flop checklist whether it flopped or not (pp 225-227).
- One sale to a stranger is a FounderOS number. Sales beyond that are their own worth-it number, not ours.
