## What this is
Products emerge from solving people's needs. We first need to choose the audience that we intend to serve. 30x500 is an amazing framework for deeply understanding an audience, communicating with them and eventually crafting a product that addresses their pains with the ultimate goal of launching a product you know will sell on day one. It all starts with choosing an audience however so this first step is where you begin. If you've already vibecoded a product set that aside for now and think about the people you want to serve.

The only rules of an audience: 
- They hang out online 
- They buy on value (ie. they have money and will pay for value to save time or make money)
- You already belong to it

There are three types of audiences: 
- Peers: existing people you could consider an equal  
- Newbies: aspiring people looking to gain proficiency in the thing 
- Clients: people who just want to buy “done for you” solutions and not actually become proficient themselves

Your job in this section is to identify the audience you want to serve. This is not a fatal choice- the only fatal move is getting paralyzed at this stage. You can always change audiences if you find the one you picked here is non-viable and you will have at that point learned Sales Safari and ruled out a non-viable option via evidence so it’s still a win.

## What you make
One short paragraph naming the group, whether you will serve peers, newcomers or clients, how you belong to the group, and one specific thing you can already help them with.

## How to submit
Write your descriptive paragraph in the box and choose the audience category.

## Done when
- Your audience is a group defined by what they do or care about, not a demographic like "parents" or a market like "small businesses".
- You say in one sentence how you belong to this group or serve it.
- You picked peers, newcomers or clients and said why.

## Coach checks
- Rule 3 honesty. A group the founder has only read about fails. The course's own failure stories are people who did not belong to their audience (pp 5, 8-9).
- "They don't hang out online" is not accepted until the next card has been tried. The course says it is usually untrue (pp 39, 59).
- An audience that does not buy on value (the course mentions hobby artists who only buy paint, wineries, house builders) gets a warning, not a block. Safari will settle it (pp 5, 39).
- Watch for agonising. If they have spent more than a few hours deciding, push them to pick one and move on (pp 38, 43).
