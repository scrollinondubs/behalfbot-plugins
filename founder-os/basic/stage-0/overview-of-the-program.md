## What this is
Most founders start with an idea. They build it, launch it, and hear crickets. Then comes the slog: customer development, pivots, chasing product-market fit, rebuilding, launching again. Lots of good builders never make it out of that loop.

30x500 flips the order. You start with people, not an idea. You pick an audience and watch them in the places they already hang out online. Amy Hoy and Alex Hillman call this Sales Safari. You learn their pains in their own words. You help them with small, free fixes, which builds rapport and gives you real empathy for what they deal with. Only then do you sell them a product, and by that point you already know they want it because they told you.

Here's what sold me on it: **30x500 is deterministic.** Most of startup building feels like a dark art. This doesn't. Follow it end to end, don't skip steps, and it works.

So how does Founder OS work? Each stage is a short set of lessons. Each lesson asks you to make something real and submit it. The coach reviews it on the spot and tells you what's missing. You fix it, it passes, and the next part opens up. Every step builds on the one before it.

Where we part ways with 30x500: Founder OS automates some things the course wants you to do by hand. An LLM is your co-pilot. It helps you make sense of your research, brainstorms with you and pokes holes in your thinking. A Chrome extension saves your Sales Safari notes so you're not copying quotes out longhand. A bot can even run parts of the safari for you.

Amy and Alex would probably have you do more of this by hand, and they have a point. Doing it manually is how you build a gut feel for a market. But most people doing this have a day job and maybe a few free evenings a week. Founder OS is the condensed version: the most effective use of the time you actually have. It pulls together the best of what I've seen work, from 30x500 and a few other sources.

Treat this as an investment. It will feel slow at first, and that's normal. **The progress compounds.** What you're really building is a muscle: the ability to parachute into any market, learn it fast and sell to it. You get to keep that one.

Last thing: the canvas. It's one page that shows how all the pieces fit together. You don't need to know the terms yet. Use it to get your bearings now and come back to it as you go.

## What you make
Nothing to write for this one. Install the Founder OS Chrome extension. It's what you'll use to save quotes when you go on Sales Safari.

## How to submit
Follow the install steps below, then come back to this page. It spots the extension and ticks this card off. On a phone, or in a browser other than Chrome, choose "continue without it" and install it later from a laptop.

## Done when
- You have read the overview and know the order: audience first, then Safari, then small free fixes, then products.
- The Founder OS Chrome extension is installed, or you chose to continue without it for now.

## Coach checks
- The app passes this card itself: it detects the extension, or the founder clicks "continue without it". It never goes to review. The submission records which path they took (meta.path is extension or override).
- If the founder skipped the extension, nudge them to install it before they start saving quotes on the Sales Safari cards.
