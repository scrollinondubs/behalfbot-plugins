## What this is
The course is blunt about it: the only way to fail is to do nothing, and showing up every week beats long sessions. Pick an amount you can actually keep, anywhere from fifteen minutes a day to a few hours a week. Small and steady wins.

## What you make
A weekly schedule for this work: which days, which hours, where, and what you will do first in each session.

## How to submit
Write your schedule in the box.

## Done when
- You have a fixed weekly time with days and hours.
- It is small enough to keep up for four straight weeks without heroics.
- You write down the first task of each session before the session starts.
- You say what you are protecting this time from, like client work or the day job.

## Coach checks
- Overcommitting is the usual mistake. The course warns against packing the day and against beating yourself up (pp 16, 19, 199-200).
- The first tasks are concrete, like "painstorm one saved thread", not "do research".
- The course suggests eight hours a week as great shape and fifteen minutes a day as a real option. One student does 50 minutes every weekday at the same time (pp 11, 16, 119). Any amount they can keep passes.
- They see this as time invested, not time spent (p 28).
