## What this is
Painstorming means reading one discussion thread slowly, top to bottom, replies included, and writing down what hurts. Read like a researcher asking what you can learn about this person's problem, not like a helper deciding what advice to give. Highlight each quote to capture it with the FounderOS Chrome extension, then write in your own words what it tells you about the person's situation and feelings.

## What you make
Captures from one real thread in one of your watering holes: the quotes that show pain, each with your note on what it implies, and a short summary of what hurts, what they want, and what they buy.

## How to submit
Install the FounderOS Chrome Extension and use it to submit your first painstorming thread.

## Done when
- You've logged your first painstorm thread via the FounderOS Chrome extension.
- Your notes hold no advice, fixes or product ideas.
- Your summary says what hurts and what they want, and marks what they buy as unknown if this thread did not show it.

## Coach checks
- Watching versus editorialising: did they capture how the poster feels, or what they would tell them? This is the course's own first critique (pp 44-45).
- The quote can be highlighted, but each note must be the student's own words about what the quote implies. A note that just repeats the quote does not count.
- Did they read the replies, and notice who comes back more than once and why they care (p 48)?
- Did they add facts of their own? Only allowed when they know it for certain, and marked as theirs (p 48).
- Pain has layers: the facts, the outcome they fear, the feelings, and what they do about it, like asking strangers, putting it off, trying shortcuts (p 50).
- Are the notes sharp and specific, not "users struggle with X" (pp 83-85)?
- Common mistake: drawing a conclusion about the whole audience from one thread (p 49).
- The course says this is hard for roughly the first fifty threads (p 50). Encourage, do not grade harshly.
