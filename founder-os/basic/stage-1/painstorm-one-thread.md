## What this is
Painstorming means reading one discussion thread slowly, top to bottom, replies included, and writing down what hurts. Read like a researcher asking what you can learn about this person's problem, not like a helper deciding what advice to give. Type each quote out in the poster's own words rather than pasting it, then note what it says about their situation and feelings.

## What you make
Notes on one real thread from one of your watering holes: its link, ten or more typed-out quotes, what each one implies, and a short summary of what hurts, what they want, and what they buy.

## How to submit
Upload your notes or share a link to them, and add the link to the thread.

## Done when
- Your notes come from one thread in one of your watering holes, with its link.
- You typed out ten or more quotes in the posters' own words, including replies, not only the first post.
- Each quote has a short note on what it says about the person's situation or feelings.
- Your notes hold no advice, fixes or product ideas.
- Your summary says what hurts and what they want, and marks what they buy as unknown if this thread did not show it.

## Coach checks
- Watching versus editorialising: did they capture how the poster feels, or what they would tell them? This is the course's own first critique (pp 44-45).
- Did they read the replies, and notice who comes back more than once and why they care (p 48)?
- Did they add facts of their own? Only allowed when they know it for certain, and marked as theirs (p 48).
- Pain has layers: the facts, the outcome they fear, the feelings, and what they do about it, like asking strangers, putting it off, trying shortcuts (p 50).
- Are the notes sharp and specific, in exact words, not "users struggle with X" (pp 83-85)?
- Common mistake: drawing a conclusion about the whole audience from one thread (p 49).
- Ten quotes is a FounderOS number. The course gives no count and says this is hard for roughly the first fifty threads (p 50).
