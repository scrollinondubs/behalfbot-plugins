## What this is
Beyond pain, collect three more things from every thread: jargon (the words insiders use), worldview (beliefs so deep the audience thinks that is just how the world works), and buys and recommendations (what they spend money, time and trust on, and what they tell each other to try). This is what makes your writing feel like a tap on the shoulder by name. Then turn the patterns into e-bomb ideas: small, free lessons that each answer one question your audience really asks.

## What you make
Notes from three or more threads across two or more watering holes, sorted under pain, jargon, worldview, and buys and recommendations, plus five or more e-bomb ideas, each pointing to the note it came from.

## How to submit
Upload your notes or share a link to them.

## Done when
- Your notes cover three or more threads from two or more different watering holes.
- All four sections (pain, jargon, worldview, buys and recommendations) have entries.
- Your jargon entries mark which words the audience would recognise instantly.
- At least one worldview entry explains the belief underneath a complaint, not just the complaint.
- You list five or more e-bomb ideas as specific questions, each tied to a quote or theme in your notes.

## Coach checks
- Are the e-bomb ideas specific enough that only this audience would click, like a question someone actually typed, not "tips for X" (pp 85, 87, 91)?
- Do they see that buying includes time, attention and trust, and that a recommendation is someone spending their reputation (p 90)?
- Worldview is the hardest section. A thin or missing worldview is the most common gap (p 88).
- Still no advice in the notes. Fixes go in the e-bomb list, not the research.
- The course blends gold into topics by taking one worldview-driven question and spinning variations (pp 95-96). Five topics from one thread is its own first exercise (p 65).
- Three threads is a FounderOS number. The course asks for more than one thread and more than one watering hole (pp 82, 88).
