## What this is
Your first e-bomb is out, so advanced mode is open. Here's the tour before you write the next four.

Three tools help you find what to write next:

- Spin this turns one e-bomb into ten or more new ideas on the same pain: where else it happens, how a belief your audience holds changes it, and what other shape the fix could take.
- The Gold tab and the Detective Board pull the pains, jargon, worldview and buys from all your notes into one place.
- The watering hole survey reads the latest titles in a place and shows what people there keep talking about. Use it to find your next place to read and share.

Then two tools make the writing better. The first is the Scorecard in the e-bomb builder. It rates an e-bomb out of 8 on Amy and Alex's four points: does it lead with crispy pain, does it deliver a fix, is there a clear next step, and does it connect the reader to the obvious next thing.

The second is Ray Edwards' headline test. **A good headline does five jobs.** It grabs attention, picks out your exact reader, pulls them into the first line, carries one big idea, and gives them a reason to trust you. He also says the way to a good headline is to write a lot of bad ones first. His five templates help:

- How-to, tied to something the reader wants.
- Transactional: do this small thing, get this result.
- Reason-why: why something happens, or a number of reasons.
- Probing question: one the reader badly wants answered.
- If-then: if you can do this easy thing, you can get this big result.

Edwards opens his sales copy with three moves, and they work for an e-bomb too. Name the person and their problem in the words they use. Show what it costs them if it stays unsolved. Then tell the story of the fix.

## What you make
Your first e-bomb scored out of 8, its headline rewritten three ways, and a short plan for the next four: four seeds from Spin this, each with its e-bomb type, plus one new watering hole.

## How to submit
Paste your score with one line on the weakest point, your three new headlines, and your four seeds in the box. Add the link to your new watering hole.

## Done when
- Your first e-bomb has a score out of 8 and one line on its weakest point.
- Three new headlines use three different templates.
- Four seeds, each a different e-bomb type and each from a different question in your notes.
- One new watering hole, found with the survey.

## Coach checks
- The score can come from the Scorecard or from the student; either is fine. Check the weakest-point line names one of the four points and says what is missing, not just "could be better".
- The three headlines should name a template each. A headline that would fit any audience fails Edwards' second job, and it fails the 30x500 rule from the first e-bomb too.
- No hype. Edwards' examples include authority claims like "Ph.D. reveals". A claim the student cannot back up fails, like any fake proof.
- Seeds that are one question reworded four ways do not count as four.
- The survey tool only shows inside advanced mode. If they stayed in beginner mode, tell them where the switch is; do not fail them for finding a watering hole by hand, but ask how they found it.
- The next card asks them to write these four. This card is the plan, not the writing.
