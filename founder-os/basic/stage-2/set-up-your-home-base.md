## What this is
The simplest recipe in 30x500 is one pain, one e-bomb, and a call to action to join your email list, shared in one watering hole. For that you need a place to publish and a list to join. You can use any tool for this but most people use either Substack, Medium or WordPress. Substack and Medium are nice because they bundle the blog publishing platform with the mailing list functions. With WordPress you have to install a plugin or integrate with an external system to get the mailing list functionality.

## What you make
A live blog or site, a working email signup form on it, and the short call to action you will put at the end of every e-bomb.

## How to submit
Share the link to your site, and write your call to action in the box.

## Done when
- Your site is live at a link anyone can open.
- You subscribed with a test address and got the confirmation email.
- Your call to action is one or two plain sentences offering more help by email, in your audience's words, with no product mentioned.
- You wrote one line on which format you will start with and why it is quick for you.

## Coach checks
- Over-building: a custom theme or a week on design is the wrong investment. The course wants the smallest step that gets a first subscriber (pp 54, 200).
- The call to action promises help, not news about the founder (p 70).
- The course offers a plain template call to action to start with (p 54). Writing your own comes later.
- Match the e-bomb format to the sort of product they are likely to sell, and start with writing (pp 97, 99).
- A link to the site belongs in their profile or signature on the watering holes, not in posts (p 110).
